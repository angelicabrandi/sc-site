Transforming Music

In today’s society, music has made an impact on people like nothing else. Music can be used in many ways, such as for healing purposes, relaxation, or entertainment: it is everywhere. However, the music industry and music consumption would not be where it is today if it were not for the Apple iPod Nano. The iPod Nano, a small compact digital music player, was introduced by Apple in 2005. The iPod Nano was the replacement for the original Apple iPod, which became a successful replacement. This new IPod transformation, the iPod Nano, became a revelation for millions of people around the world and for music consumption because of its portability, personalization, and modern-day impact.

The iPod Nano was a part of the new, groundbreaking, technological advancements in the early 2000s because of its portability. Before the iPod Nano, music could be listened to on the go on the radio or on Sony’s Walkman, but the radio was only accessible in a home or vehicle and the Walkman was a clunky portable audio player. The iPod Nano is a pocket friendly device that allows people to listen to music on the go. In comparison to the original iPod Mini, the iPod Nano is 80% smaller in size and 1.5 ounces (about 48 grams) (Frommer). The iPod Nano was so small that you could “[sit] at the back of the classroom listening to music with your friends while you were supposed to be doing math,” and no one would ever know because of how compact it is (Brain). Additionally, the iPod Nano can be used with ease. The simplicity of this device allows for use across many generations. The iPod Nano consisted of a few applications and one dial, which made the device extremely easy to use. This transformed the music industry and overall music consumption because people could now easily listen to music anywhere at any time, since it is right in their pocket.

Another aspect of the iPod Nano that was groundbreaking was the idea of personalization. With this device, for the first time, people were able to personalize music by creating their own playlists and soundtracks. Unlike the radio, where people are limited to specific stations, the iPod Nano carries thousands of songs, specific for its owner. Other than creating playlists on iTunes, the device had other applications that could further personalize the device, such as clock, photos, games, calendar, stopwatch/times, and a screen lock (Frommer). Additionally, Apple allowed for further personalization by introducing colors, such as red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink, and silver. Originally, when the iPod Nano was first released, it had a price of $249, 4G of storage, and was offered in black or white (Frommer). This element of personalization offered a whole new experience with music: something the music industry has never seen before.

In recent years, the impact of the iPod Nano has been more prevalent than ever before, due to the rise in mental health awareness. The article “How Music Helps People Heal: The Therapeutic Power of Music” by David Victor, Victor goes into detail about how music has a healing power for people, since it can be used as a form of therapy. Scientific studies have shown that “music can reduce stress, anxiety, depression, and even chronic physical pain” by releasing endorphins and dopamine hormones (Victor). Listening to music can be used as a form of self-expression. People can communicate their emotions through several types of music; for example, “listening to uplifting songs or singing along with them can help boost our moods” (Victor). I relate to this completely, as do many other people, because the minute I am feeling down or not feeling like myself, I resort to music: within minutes I feel better. I believe that music would nto have the effect or impact it has today if the iPod Nano was not created. The iPod Nano allowed people to implement music into every moment of their life, giving music a deeper and more emotional meaning.

Therefore, the iPod Nano was a crucial part of the transformation of the music industry, mainly because of its portability and personalization. Although the iPod Nano was discontinued July 27, 2017, it had left a major impact on the music industry. Today, there are many different types of streaming platforms right at our fingertips, such as Apple Music, Pandora, and Spotify, with a $10 monthly subscription payment. Personally, I believe the iPod Nano was the foundation for the music industry we have today. Music plays an exceptional role in people's day-to-day lives. Music is an outlet. No matter the circumstances in a person's life, music will always be there, within reach. I listen to all different genres of music, such as country, pop, R&B, or rap, depending on my mood, which puts my mind at ease. Personally, I do not think the music industry and music consumption would not be where it is today if it were not for Apple's release of the iPod Nano in 2005.

Healing Through Music

As a society, we are completely surrounded by music, whether that would be listening to the radio while driving, going to the grocery store while it plays in the background, scrolling on social media, or going to a workout class: there is no escaping it. Music has been around for thousands of years, yet, due to its running relevance, it continues to evolve and develop. The most noticeable development of music is the different genres of music, such as jazz, rhythm and blues, rock, pop, rap, and country, which have stemmed from musical trends in specific decades. Previously, music has only been recognized for its entertainment purposes. In recent years, through the evolution of music technology, music has been recognized for its profound health benefits. Music technology started from a record player to cassette tapes and radios, CDs, walkmans, MP3 players, and finally streaming platforms. Due to the recent accessibility of music, scientific data has supported that music has significant benefits for health, more specifically, cognitive development and overall emotional well-being: in both children and adults.

In regards to cognitive development, which is the process of how people's mental abilities develop, such as thinking, reasoning, understanding, growth, and change, music has shown significant developmental improvements. These developments are especially important in the early years of a child’s life because those first few years are the peak of a child’s learning, thinking, and growth. According to the Starts Hear campaign, hearing is the most important sense when it comes to brain development, as it “builds a foundation for learning, spoken language, and literacy” (Starts Hear Campaign). One way to implement hearing activities into a child’s daily routine is to allow the child to listen to music daily. Young children love to engage with musical activities, so exposure to music in early childhood is an easy and accessible way to aid in cognitive development. In the article “The Importance of Music in Early Childhood” by University of Maryland College of Education, the authors explain to their readers how music is a form of “sensory input which can promote cognitive development” (University of Maryland College of Education). Within each piece of music, there are many different components to it, such as the melody, lyrics, tempo, and dynamics. These different components of each piece can help young children “to anticipate patterns and place objects or events in sequence helps build critical early math and early reading skills (University of Maryland College of Education). Music has revealed numerous benefits to the cognitive development of young children: memory, attention, creativity, language, and motor skills.

Although cognitive development subsides as one gets older, it is considered to be a lifelong process. As one gets older, the risk of developing dementia, a chronic disease that causes a major decline in one’s cognitive abilities, increases tremendously. The loss of memory is the most apparent symptom of dementia. In order to aid this memory loss, music has become a prominent source of therapy for brain impairments. In the article "How Music Affects Memory and Concentration" by Levine Music, the author educates the reader on how music impacts the brain positively with memory recall. For example, the author states,“Studies have shown that ‘music doesn’t just help us retrieve stored memories, it also helps us lay down new ones.’ A tune connected to an event or information forms a kind of soundtrack, which, when replayed, can spark a recollection” (Levine Music). By playing a specific tune, one can associate the tune to a specific memory: aiding in memory recall. Using this strategy of music therapy on people with brain impairments can make recalling memories slightly easier for them. In addition to memory recall, music can aid in focus and concentration. People suffering with dementia will also have increasing difficulties in concentration and focus: along with the other symptoms. When performing a task, listening to slow and/or instrumental music “minimizes distraction, helps maintain a consistent work pace, and can even make tasks more enjoyable” (Levine Music). Moreover, music is extremely important in cognitive development. Even though aiding in cognitive development is important and urgent in children, it is just as important for adults. Due to the continuous rise in music, the music industry has made music easily accessible through streaming platforms, allowing people to implement music into their lives whenever and wherever they want.

In addition to cognitive development, music can benefit one's emotional well-being by reducing stress and anxiety and boosting your overall mood. In today’s society, there has been a rise in mental health related illnesses. As a society, it is important to recognize the healing and therapeutic mechanism that are just in reach to us: music. The article “How Music Helps People Heal: The Therapeutic Power of Music” by David Victor, the author explores the healing mechanisms music embodies: for both physical and mental pain. For physical pain, music has been used as a form of medical therapy. For example, Victor exclaims, “Studies by scientists have also found that listening to music helps release endorphins – hormones associated with pleasure (and reducing pain) – which further reduces stress levels and may even aid in the healing process” (Victor). Using music in a medical setting can offer the patients, specifically people with chronic medical conditions, a more stress-free environment, reducing their overall physical pain. Similarly, music can be used for mental health therapy because “listening to uplifting songs or singing along with them can help boost our moods by increasing serotonin levels in the brain – the neurotransmitter responsible for regulating our emotions and feelings of happiness or sadness” (Victor). Personally, I find listening to music can relieve my overall stress and lighten my mood. I have created different playlists based on my different moods, each of these various playlists containing different genres of music including pop, country, blues, and other styles of music. Music is easily accessible to us, so it is important that we take advantage of its benefits. However, music would not be so easily accessible if Apple did not create the iPod Nano in 2005. The iPod Nano was a revolutionary technological advancement for both the music industry as well as music consumption. MP3 players, such as the iPod Nano, were the first devices to allow people to bring music on the go: implementing music into every moment of people’s daily lives. Although MP3 players could be taken on the go, there were some limitations, such as the number of songs that could be downloaded onto the device. These complications led to the development of streaming platforms, Apple Music and Spotify, making music the most accessible it has ever been. No matter the circumstances of someone’s health, mentally or physically, music is always right there to heal us.

Therefore, music holds the power and capabilities to boost cognitive development and improve overall health in both children and adults. Earning itself the title of being the “universal language,” music is breaking barriers and becoming more prominent than ever. As consumers of music, it's important for us, as a society, to recognize the benefits of listening to music. Due to the evolution and development of musical technology, music has become super accessible to us: it is right at our fingertips. Without previous technologies, like the iPod Nano, music would not be where it is today.
